# Test: planning step, IKEA PAX closet (2026-11-21)

Job: "I want a PAX wardrobe in the bedroom", old Copenhagen flat, first time. This tests steps 1-2 (no prices). The point: the user doesn't know the tools or the traps.

**Questions asked (one at a time):** width and height of the wall? Ceiling height? Wall type (brick, or plaster on lath)? Tools you own?
Answers used: 200 cm wall, ceiling 250 cm, brick outer wall, owns a cordless drill.

**Ways to do it:**
1. PAX 2 × 100 cm, 236 cm high, fixed to the wall. 1 day, ~4-6,000 kr. **Recommended.**
2. PAX 201 cm high: easier to raise, less storage. Pick this if the ceiling is under ~245 cm.
3. Built-in with a carpenter: 3× the price.

**Plan:**
1. Check the frame can stand up. It is assembled lying down and tilted up, so the ceiling must beat the frame's diagonal: √(236² + 58²) ≈ 243 cm. With 250 cm, OK. *Mistake: building it standing up, which can't be done alone.*
2. Check the floor with a spirit level. Old floors slope, so plan shims under the low side.
3. Assemble the frames lying on the carton (protects the floor). Two people for tilting.
4. Fix to the wall. Brick needs a hammer drill, 6 mm masonry bit and brick plugs. *Mistake: the plugs in the IKEA box, which don't suit Danish brick or lath.*
5. Fit doors and hinges, then adjust them last, when the frame no longer moves.

**Tools:** hammer drill (most cordless drills can't do brick: rent it, Hygglo first), spirit level 60+ cm, rubber mallet, step ladder, stud/pipe detector (check before drilling).
**Materials:** 8 × 6 mm brick plugs with 4.5-5 × 60 screws, shims.

**Result:** the plan names the 3 things a first-timer misses (ceiling diagonal, uneven floor, wall plugs) and only one tool to rent. Sourcing then runs on that one tool and the plugs.
